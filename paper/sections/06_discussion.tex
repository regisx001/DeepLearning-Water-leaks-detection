\section{Discussion}

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CNN-BiLSTM-Attention
architecture is effective for detecting water leaks in multivariate time-series
data. The model achieves high AUC-ROC and AUC-PR scores, indicating strong
discriminative capability even in the presence of class imbalance. In addition,
the selected operating point provides a favorable balance between precision and
recall, with a low false alarm rate and a high detection rate for leak events.

A key strength of the proposed approach lies in its ability to capture both
local and long-range temporal dependencies. The convolutional layers extract
short-term patterns such as abrupt pressure drops or flow spikes, while the
BiLSTM component models temporal relationships across the sequence. The
self-attention mechanism further enhances the model by assigning higher
importance to informative time steps, allowing it to focus on critical segments
associated with leak events.

Another important contribution is the use of threshold calibration to control
the trade-off between detection sensitivity and false alarms. Rather than relying
on a fixed decision threshold, the model can be tuned to meet specific operational
requirements. For example, in safety-critical environments, a lower threshold may
be preferred to maximize recall, while in cost-sensitive scenarios, a higher
threshold may be used to reduce false alarms. This flexibility makes the proposed
framework suitable for real-world deployment.

The analysis of score distributions and threshold sensitivity further confirms
that the model produces well-separated probability outputs for leak and normal
classes. This indicates that the learned representations are meaningful and that
the classification boundary is well-defined. Moreover, the low number of false
positives observed in the confusion matrix suggests that the model avoids
over-triggering alarms, which is a common issue in anomaly detection systems.

Despite these promising results, several limitations should be acknowledged.
First, the dataset used in this study is limited to a specific monitoring period
and network configuration, which may restrict the generalization of the model to
other environments. Second, while the model demonstrates strong performance, it
has not been compared against a wide range of baseline methods, which would
provide a more comprehensive evaluation. Third, the deep learning model operates
as a black-box system, and although attention mechanisms provide some level of
interpretability, further analysis is needed to fully understand the decision
process.

Future work will focus on addressing these limitations by evaluating the model
on additional datasets, incorporating baseline comparisons, and exploring more
interpretable architectures. In addition, extending the framework to support
leak localization and severity estimation would further enhance its practical
utility in smart water management systems.

Overall, the results highlight the effectiveness of combining convolutional,
recurrent, and attention-based mechanisms for time-series leak detection, and
demonstrate the importance of integrating data processing, modeling, and
decision calibration into a unified framework.
