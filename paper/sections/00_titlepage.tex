% Abstract
\begin{leftbar}
\textbf{\textcolor{headingblue}{ABSTRACT}} This paper presents an end-to-end
deep learning framework for automatic leak detection in urban water distribution
networks using multivariate sensor time-series data. The proposed approach
integrates domain-informed feature engineering, sliding-window sequence modeling,
and a hybrid architecture combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN),
Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM), and a self-attention mechanism.

The CNN component captures local temporal patterns, the BiLSTM models long-range
dependencies, and the attention mechanism highlights the most informative time
steps within each sequence. In addition, a leakage-free temporal data splitting
strategy and threshold calibration are employed to ensure robust and realistic
evaluation under class imbalance.

Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves strong
performance, with an AUC-PR of $0.9583$ and an F1-score of $0.9318$, while
maintaining high recall. Furthermore, benchmark comparisons against classical
machine learning models and alternative deep learning architectures confirm
the effectiveness of the proposed hybrid model.

Overall, the proposed framework provides a practical and scalable solution for
real-world smart water monitoring systems, contributing to improved infrastructure
reliability and reduced water loss.
\end{leftbar}
