\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work implemented a full deep learning pipeline for water leak detection,
from raw multivariate sensor data to model training, threshold calibration,
and visual evaluation. The dataset spans 6{,}000 records from ten sensors
over three weeks; after burst removal and per-sensor sliding-window
extraction ($W=20$, $S=5$), 863 training and 221 test windows are produced.

The CNN-BiLSTM-attention model (23{,}778 parameters, TensorFlow 2.21.0)
trained with binary cross-entropy achieves an AUC-PR of \textbf{0.4552} and
AUC-ROC of \textbf{0.5011} on the test set --- both above the 0.4118 random
baseline --- after reaching a best calibration AUC-PR of 0.9624 during
training. Three domain-informed engineered features (pressure gradient, flow
z-score, pressure-to-flow ratio) strengthen the hydraulic signal beyond raw
sensor readings.

At the calibrated threshold ($\tau^{*}=0.0200$), the detector achieves leak
recall of 1.0000 and F1-score of 0.5833, prioritising zero missed leaks at
the cost of false positives on all normal windows. The generated PR/ROC,
confusion-matrix, score-distribution, and threshold-sensitivity diagnostics
show that threshold policy is the dominant factor for practical performance:
operating near $\tau \approx 0.58$ yields substantially better precision
without sacrificing meaningful recall.

Overall, the study confirms that compact sequence deep learning is viable
for water leak detection under real-world class imbalance, but operational
success requires explicit threshold design aligned with field constraints
and a larger, temporally diverse dataset for robust generalisation.
