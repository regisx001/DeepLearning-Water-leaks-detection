\section{Conclusion}

\noindent This paper presented an end-to-end deep learning framework for automatic
water leak detection in urban distribution networks using multivariate
time-series sensor data. The proposed approach integrates domain-informed
feature engineering, sliding-window sequence construction, and a hybrid
CNN-BiLSTM-Attention architecture to effectively capture both local and
long-range temporal dependencies.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent Experimental results demonstrate that the model achieves strong performance,
with a favorable balance between precision, recall, and false alarm rate.
Benchmark comparisons further confirm that the proposed model provides superior
operational performance compared to classical and deep learning baselines,
highlighting the effectiveness of the hybrid architecture.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent The results emphasize the importance of combining advanced modeling
techniques with robust data preprocessing, leakage-free evaluation, and
threshold calibration to develop reliable and deployable monitoring systems.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent Future work will focus on evaluating the model on more diverse datasets,
extending the benchmark with additional approaches, and improving
interpretability. Furthermore, integrating leak localization and severity
estimation represents a promising direction for enhancing practical applicability.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent Overall, this work contributes to the development of intelligent and
reliable solutions for urban water monitoring, supporting improved infrastructure
management and reduced water loss.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent \textbf{Potential Applications.}
The proposed framework can be integrated into Internet of Things (IoT)-based
smart water management systems for real-time leak detection and monitoring.
By continuously processing sensor data streams, the model can provide early
warnings of leak events, enabling faster response and reducing water loss.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent In addition, the system can be extended to support automated control mechanisms,
such as dynamic valve management. In the event of a detected leak, the model
can trigger automatic valve closure or flow regulation to isolate affected
segments of the network, thereby minimizing damage and operational disruption.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent Such integration enables the transition from reactive maintenance to proactive
and autonomous infrastructure management, improving system reliability and
operational efficiency. The proposed approach therefore represents a key
building block for next-generation smart water distribution systems.
