\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Traditional Leak Detection Methods}
Early approaches to leak detection in water distribution systems relied
primarily on hydraulic modeling and pressure transient analysis
\cite{colombo2009review}. These methods simulate expected pressure
profiles under normal operating conditions and flag deviations exceeding
a fixed threshold. While effective in well-instrumented networks with
accurate topology models, they require detailed knowledge of pipe
geometry, material properties, and demand patterns --- information
that is rarely available in its entirety for real urban networks.
Acoustic correlation methods improved physical localization accuracy
but demand costly specialized hardware and skilled personnel for
interpretation, limiting their scalability.

\subsection{Statistical and Shallow Machine Learning Methods}
The emergence of SCADA systems and permanent pressure monitoring
enabled data-driven approaches. Statistical process control methods,
including CUSUM and EWMA charts, were applied to detect sustained
shifts in pressure or flow signals \cite{mounce2011novelty}.
Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forests were subsequently
evaluated on engineered feature sets derived from pressure and
flow measurements, showing improved detection rates over threshold
rules. However, these methods treat each timestep as an independent
observation, discarding the temporal context that is central to
leak behavior. Feature engineering in these works is manual and
domain-specific, limiting transferability across different network
configurations.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Time-Series Classification}
Convolutional Neural Networks applied to raw 1D time-series have
achieved state-of-the-art results across multiple industrial
fault detection tasks \cite{lei2020applications}. The key advantage
over shallow methods is automatic feature extraction: convolutional
filters learn discriminative local patterns directly from the data
without manual engineering. Fawaz et al. \cite{fawaz2019deep}
conducted a comprehensive benchmark of deep learning architectures
for time-series classification, establishing 1D-CNN and ResNet
variants as strong baselines across 85 datasets.

Recurrent architectures, particularly LSTM \cite{hochreiter1997long}
and its bidirectional variant (BiLSTM), address the sequential
dependency structure of time-series data that CNNs cannot fully
capture with fixed-size kernels. Karim et al. \cite{karim2018lstm}
demonstrated that hybrid LSTM-FCN models, combining recurrent
and convolutional pathways in parallel, outperform either
architecture in isolation on sensor classification benchmarks.
These findings motivate our sequential CNN-BiLSTM design, where
the convolutional block feeds into the recurrent block rather
than operating in parallel.

\subsection{Attention Mechanisms in Time-Series}
The self-attention mechanism, introduced in the Transformer
architecture for natural language processing \cite{vaswani2017attention},
has been adapted for time-series tasks to assign learned scalar
importance weights to individual timesteps. Unlike global average
pooling, attention-weighted aggregation allows the model to
selectively focus on the most diagnostic temporal positions,
improving both predictive performance and interpretability.
Several works have incorporated attention into hybrid CNN-LSTM
models for industrial anomaly detection, reporting consistent
improvements in F1-score and providing heatmap-style explanations
that align with domain expert expectations.

\subsection{Class Imbalance in Anomaly Detection}
Leak and fault events are inherently rare in healthy operating
systems, creating severe class imbalance that standard training
objectives fail to handle. Oversampling techniques such as SMOTE
\cite{chawla2002smote} generate synthetic minority samples but
risk introducing unrealistic interpolations in multivariate
sensor space. Cost-sensitive learning assigns higher misclassification
penalties to the minority class, improving recall at the cost of
precision. Lin et al. \cite{lin2017focal} proposed Focal Loss as
an elegant alternative: by dynamically down-weighting the loss
contribution of easy, correctly-classified majority samples, it
focuses gradient updates on hard, misclassified minority examples
without requiring data augmentation. We adopt Focal Loss in this
work, combined with decision threshold tuning on the validation
set, as the primary strategy for handling the 98.1\%/1.9\%
Normal/Leak imbalance in our dataset.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Water Networks}
Recent works have begun applying deep learning specifically to
water distribution anomaly detection. Autoencoder-based approaches
model normal operating patterns and flag high reconstruction error
as anomalous \cite{hu2021review}, but require careful calibration
of the reconstruction threshold and provide no temporal
interpretability. Supervised CNN and LSTM models have been
evaluated on the BattLeDIM benchmark dataset \cite{vrachimis2020battledim},
confirming the superiority of deep sequential models over
threshold-based baselines. Our work extends this line of research
by combining the complementary strengths of CNN, BiLSTM, and
attention in a unified supervised architecture, while explicitly
addressing class imbalance through Focal Loss.
