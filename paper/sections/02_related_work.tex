\section{Related Work}

Automatic leak detection in water distribution networks has been widely studied,
with approaches ranging from traditional signal processing techniques to modern
data-driven machine learning models. Existing methods can be broadly categorized
into three groups: physics-based approaches, classical machine learning methods,
and deep learning-based models.

\subsection{Traditional and Physics-Based Methods}

Early work in leak detection primarily relied on hydraulic modeling and
signal processing techniques. Methods based on pressure residual analysis,
acoustic sensing, and flow balance estimation have been widely used in practice.
These approaches typically depend on domain-specific knowledge and require
accurate physical models of the water network. While effective in controlled
environments, they often struggle in real-world scenarios due to noise,
uncertainty, and the complexity of urban infrastructure systems.

\subsection{Machine Learning Approaches}

With the increasing availability of sensor data, machine learning techniques
have been introduced for leak detection tasks. Classical models such as
Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forests, and Gradient Boosting have been
applied to classify normal and anomalous behavior based on extracted features.
These approaches improve scalability compared to rule-based systems but often
require extensive feature engineering and may fail to capture temporal
dependencies inherent in time-series data.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Time-Series Leak Detection}

Recent advances in deep learning have enabled more effective modeling of
multivariate time-series data. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) have been
used to capture local temporal patterns, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN),
particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, are capable of modeling
long-term dependencies.

Several studies have explored hybrid architectures combining CNN and LSTM
to leverage both spatial and temporal feature extraction. These models have
demonstrated improved performance compared to standalone approaches, especially
in complex monitoring environments.

More recently, attention mechanisms have been introduced to enhance sequence
modeling by allowing models to focus on the most relevant time steps. Attention
has been successfully applied in various time-series applications, improving both
performance and interpretability. However, its application to water leak
detection remains relatively limited.

\subsection{Limitations of Existing Work}

Despite these advances, several limitations remain. Many existing studies do not
adequately address class imbalance, which is a critical challenge in leak
detection where leak events are relatively rare. Furthermore, evaluation is often
limited to accuracy-based metrics, which may not reflect real-world operational
requirements such as minimizing false alarms.

In addition, some approaches do not ensure strict separation between training and
test data in temporal settings, potentially leading to data leakage and
over-optimistic performance estimates.

\subsection{Proposed Approach}

To address these limitations, this paper proposes a hybrid CNN-BiLSTM architecture
augmented with a self-attention mechanism for multivariate time-series leak
detection. In contrast to prior work, the proposed approach integrates:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A combination of CNN and BiLSTM to capture both local and long-range temporal patterns,
    \item A self-attention mechanism to identify the most informative time steps,
    \item A leakage-free temporal data splitting strategy,
    \item An imbalance-aware evaluation framework using AUC-PR and threshold calibration.
\end{itemize}

This combination enables robust and reliable leak detection while maintaining a
balanced trade-off between detection sensitivity and false alarm rate.
