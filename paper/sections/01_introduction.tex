\section{Introduction}

\lettrine[lines=3, findent=0.1em, nindent=0em]{\textbf{W}}{ater} distribution networks
are critical infrastructure systems that supply potable water to millions of people
worldwide. However, these networks suffer from significant water losses due to
\textbf{leaks and bursts} in aging pipelines. According to the World Bank, an estimated
32 billion cubic meters of treated water are lost annually through leaks, representing
approximately 30--50\% of the total water supplied in many developing countries. These
losses translate into substantial economic costs, energy waste, and increased risk of
water contamination.

\subsection{Global Context}

The scale of the water loss problem varies significantly across regions. In developed
countries such as Japan and Germany, non-revenue water (NRW) rates are as low as
5--10\%, thanks to modern infrastructure and proactive maintenance programs. In contrast,
many countries in Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia experience NRW rates exceeding
40\%, with some cities losing more than 60\% of their treated water before it reaches
consumers.

In \textbf{Morocco}, the challenge is particularly acute. According to the Moroccan
Office National de l'Electricité et de l'Eau Potable (ONEE), the national NRW rate is
estimated at approximately 28--35\%, with some rural and peri-urban networks reaching
50\%. The country's arid and semi-arid climate makes every cubic meter of water precious,
and the growing demand driven by urbanization and agricultural irrigation further amplifies
the urgency. The city of \textbf{Tangier}, home to the Faculty of Sciences and Techniques
(FST), faces particular water stress due to its rapid population growth and the limitations
of the surrounding dam infrastructure.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

Traditional approaches to leak detection rely on \textbf{manual inspection}, acoustic
sensors, and \textbf{threshold-based alarm systems}. While these methods can detect
large-scale burst events, they often fail to identify small, gradual leaks that develop
over time. Such early-stage leaks are particularly challenging because they produce
\textbf{subtle, sustained deviations} in sensor readings---pressure drops, flow rate
changes, and temperature variations---that are difficult to distinguish from normal
operational fluctuations.

The fundamental difficulty can be formalized as follows: given a multivariate time series
$\mathbf{X} = \{\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{x}_T\}$ where each
$\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ represents $d$ sensor measurements at time $t$, the task
is to predict a binary label $y_t \in \{0, 1\}$ indicating whether a leak is occurring.
The challenge lies in the fact that leak-induced deviations may be smaller than the
natural variance of the signals, making point-wise classification unreliable. Instead,
the model must learn to recognize \textbf{temporal patterns}---sequences of subtle,
correlated changes across multiple sensor channels---that are characteristic of leak events.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Time-Series Anomaly Detection}

Recent advances in \textbf{Machine Learning (ML)} and \textbf{Deep Learning (DL)} have
opened new possibilities for automated anomaly detection in time-series data. In
particular, \textbf{Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)} have shown remarkable ability
to extract local temporal patterns from sequential data, making them well-suited for
detecting the characteristic signatures of water leaks in sensor measurements. Unlike
traditional methods, deep learning models can learn complex, non-linear relationships
between multiple sensor signals simultaneously.

The key advantage of 1D-CNNs over classical approaches is their ability to perform
\textbf{automatic feature extraction}. Rather than requiring domain experts to manually
design features (e.g., rolling averages, peak detection, spectral analysis), the
convolutional filters learn to detect discriminative patterns directly from raw data.
This property is particularly valuable in water distribution networks, where the
signatures of different leak types may vary depending on pipe material, diameter,
pressure regime, and soil conditions.

\subsection{Contributions}

In this study, we propose a \textbf{1D-CNN-based approach} for automatic water leak
detection using multivariate time-series data from pressure, flow rate, and temperature
sensors deployed across an urban water distribution network. Our methodology involves:
(1) transforming raw sensor data into \textbf{sliding windows} to capture temporal
context, (2) applying \textbf{feature normalization} for stable training, (3) training a
compact CNN with \textbf{batch normalization} and \textbf{balanced class weights} to
handle the inherent class imbalance, and (4) evaluating with \textbf{sensor-wise
GroupKFold cross-validation} to ensure no data leakage between training and validation
sets. The results demonstrate that our approach provides a solid baseline for leak
detection, while highlighting the challenges of working with small-scale sensor datasets.

Beyond the model itself, this work makes a \textbf{methodological contribution} by
documenting the common pitfalls encountered during development---including data leakage
through overlapping windows, label amplification from windowed labeling, and the
overfitting risks inherent in small time-series datasets---and presenting principled
solutions to each.
