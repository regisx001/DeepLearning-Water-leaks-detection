\section{Introduction}

\lettrine[lines=2]{\color{titleblue}W}{ater} distribution networks form a
critical pillar of urban infrastructure worldwide. Despite decades of
engineering advances, physical losses caused by undetected pipe leaks
remain a persistent and costly challenge. The World Bank estimates that
non-revenue water loss in developing countries regularly exceeds 30\% of
total treated supply \cite{worldbank2006}, imposing severe economic burdens
on municipalities and contributing to water scarcity in regions already
under hydric stress.

Conventional leak detection relies on periodic manual inspection, acoustic
correlators, or simple pressure threshold alarms. These approaches share a
fundamental limitation: they are \textit{reactive}, triggering only after a
leak has already caused measurable infrastructure damage or significant water
loss. Furthermore, threshold-based rules fail to distinguish genuine leak
events from normal pressure fluctuations caused by demand variation, pump
cycling, or sensor drift, leading to high false-positive rates in practice.

The widespread deployment of low-cost IoT pressure and flow sensors in modern
smart water networks now makes it possible to collect dense, continuous
time-series data from distribution systems. This creates an opportunity for
\textit{predictive} anomaly detection: learning the subtle temporal signatures
that precede or accompany a leak event before the situation becomes critical.
Machine learning methods have begun to exploit this data, yet most prior work
relies on hand-crafted statistical features or shallow classifiers that treat
each sensor reading independently, ignoring the temporal context that is
central to leak behavior \cite{mounce2011novelty}.

Deep learning architectures, particularly 1D Convolutional Neural Networks
(CNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, have demonstrated
state-of-the-art performance on multivariate time-series classification
tasks across multiple industrial domains \cite{fawaz2019deep}. CNNs excel
at extracting local patterns such as sharp pressure drops, while recurrent
architectures model sequential dependencies that unfold over multiple
timesteps. Hybrid CNN-LSTM models combine both strengths and have
outperformed either architecture in isolation on sensor-based anomaly
detection benchmarks \cite{karim2018lstm}.

Nevertheless, applying deep learning to water leak detection faces two
practical challenges that prior work has not fully addressed. First,
\textbf{extreme class imbalance}: leak events are inherently rare, typically
representing less than 2\% of sensor readings in a healthy network.
Standard training objectives collapse to predicting the majority class,
achieving high nominal accuracy while missing every actual leak. Second,
\textbf{limited interpretability}: black-box predictions are insufficient
in operational contexts where infrastructure engineers require an
explanation of \textit{when} and \textit{why} the model raised an alarm
before committing to an expensive field inspection.

This paper addresses both challenges through the following contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\arabic*.]
    \item We propose a \textbf{CNN-BiLSTM with Self-Attention} architecture
          that captures local pressure-drop patterns via convolutional layers,
          long-range temporal dependencies via a Bidirectional LSTM, and
          produces timestep-level importance scores via a lightweight attention
          module.

    \item We introduce \textbf{three engineered features} --- pressure delta
          $\Delta P_t$, per-sensor flow Z-score $Z_{F,t}$, and pressure-flow
          ratio $R_t$ --- that substantially improve class separability beyond
          raw sensor readings and are physically motivated by hydraulic leak
          dynamics.

    \item We apply \textbf{Focal Loss} \cite{lin2017focal} with a tuned
          focusing parameter $\gamma = 2.0$ and minority-class weight
          $\alpha = 0.75$ to resolve the 98.1\%/1.9\% imbalance without
          requiring oversampling or synthetic data generation.

    \item We provide \textbf{attention weight visualizations} over leak-positive
          windows, demonstrating that the model consistently attends to the
          timesteps immediately preceding the pressure drop, aligning with
          the physical dynamics of leak onset and offering operational
          interpretability to field engineers.
\end{enumerate}
