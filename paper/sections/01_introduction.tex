\section{Introduction}

\lettrine[lines=2]{\color{titleblue}L}{eak} detection in water distribution
networks is a critical task for reducing water losses, operating costs, and
service disruptions. In real monitoring systems, leaks are rare events that
must be detected from noisy multivariate time-series signals (pressure, flow,
temperature) collected across multiple sensors. This setting creates two
practical difficulties: strong class imbalance and ambiguous temporal patterns
that are not well captured by static threshold rules.

This project develops and evaluates a deep learning pipeline for automated leak
detection from sensor windows. The model combines three components: (1) 1D
convolution layers for local temporal patterns, (2) a bidirectional LSTM for
sequence context, and (3) temporal attention for weighted aggregation across
timesteps. The pipeline is paired with feature engineering and calibration-based
decision thresholding to support deployment-oriented trade-off analysis.

The main contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\arabic*.]
    \item A complete reproducible workflow from raw CSV data to model training,
          evaluation, and publication-ready figures.
    \item A compact CNN-BiLSTM-attention architecture trained with binary
          cross-entropy for leak classification.
    \item A calibrated operating threshold analysis that highlights precision,
          recall, and false-alarm trade-offs for operational usage.
    \item A LaTeX paper structure aligned with generated experimental figures
          for direct reporting.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related}
summarizes the relevant leak-detection and time-series modeling context.
Section~\ref{sec:dataset} details the dataset, exploratory analysis, and
preprocessing. Section~\ref{sec:methodology} presents the implemented training
pipeline and model architecture. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports quantitative
results with figure-based analysis. Sections~\ref{sec:discussion} and
\ref{sec:conclusion} discuss implications, limitations, and next steps.
