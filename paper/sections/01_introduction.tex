\section{Introduction}

\lettrine[lines=2]{\color{titleblue}L}{eak} detection in water distribution
networks is a critical task for reducing water losses, operating costs, and
service disruptions. In real monitoring systems, leaks are rare events that
must be detected from noisy multivariate time-series signals --- pressure,
flow rate, and temperature --- collected across multiple geographically
distributed sensors. This setting creates two practical difficulties: strong
class imbalance (leak events represented fewer than 14\,\% of records after
cleaning) and ambiguous temporal patterns that are not well captured by
static threshold rules or single-timestep classifiers.

This project develops and evaluates a deep learning pipeline for automated
leak detection from multivariate sensor windows. The model combines three
components: (1)~1D convolution layers that extract local temporal motifs
such as sudden pressure drops, (2)~a bidirectional LSTM that aggregates
sequence context in both temporal directions, and (3)~temporal attention that
produces an importance-weighted summary of the window. The pipeline is paired
with domain-informed feature engineering and calibration-based decision
thresholding to support deployment-oriented trade-off analysis.

The experimental data comprise 6{,}000 readings from ten sensors spanning
three weeks (2024-01-01 to 2024-01-21). After burst exclusion, 5{,}590
records are retained and transformed into 1{,}084 labelled windows for
training and test evaluation.

The main contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=\arabic*.]
\item A complete reproducible workflow from raw CSV data to model training,
evaluation, and publication-ready figures, implemented across five Jupyter
notebooks.
\item A compact CNN-BiLSTM-attention architecture (23{,}778 parameters) trained
with binary cross-entropy for binary leak/normal window classification.
\item Three domain-informed engineered features --- pressure gradient,
sensor-normalised flow z-score, and pressure-to-flow ratio --- grounded in
hydraulic theory and validated through correlation analysis
($r_{\text{Pressure, Leak}} = -0.606$).
\item A calibrated operating threshold analysis ($\tau^{*} = 0.0200$) that
highlights precision, recall, and false-alarm trade-offs for operational usage.
\end{enumerate}
