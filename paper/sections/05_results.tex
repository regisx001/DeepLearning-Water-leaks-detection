\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The proposed model is evaluated on a temporally separated test set to ensure
realistic performance assessment and avoid data leakage. The dataset is split
into training, calibration, and test subsets, where the calibration set is used
for threshold optimization.

Performance is evaluated using metrics suitable for imbalanced classification,
including AUC-ROC, AUC-PR, precision, recall, F1-score, false alarm rate, and
miss rate.

\subsection{Training Behavior}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig6_training_curves.png}
\caption{Training and validation curves showing loss and AUC-PR evolution.}
\label{fig:training_curves}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:training_curves} shows the evolution of training and validation
metrics during optimization. The model demonstrates stable convergence, with
validation AUC-PR improving steadily before reaching a plateau. The absence of
significant divergence between training and validation curves indicates that
overfitting is effectively controlled.

\subsection{Model Performance}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig7_pr_roc_curves.png}
\caption{Precision-Recall and ROC curves on the test set.}
\label{fig:pr_roc}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:pr_roc} presents the Precision-Recall (PR) and ROC curves
evaluated on the test set. The model achieves an AUC-ROC of $0.9412$ and an
AUC-PR of $0.9583$, indicating strong discriminative performance, particularly
in the presence of class imbalance.

The high AUC-PR value confirms that the model maintains high precision across
a wide range of recall levels, which is critical for reliable leak detection.

\subsection{Confusion Matrix Analysis}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\columnwidth]{images/fig8_confusion_matrix.png}
\caption{Confusion matrix at the selected operating threshold.}
\label{fig:confusion_matrix}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:confusion_matrix} illustrates the confusion matrix obtained at
the selected operating threshold $\tau = 0.0333$. The model correctly identifies
82 leak instances and 127 normal instances, while producing only 3 false positives
and 9 false negatives.

This corresponds to a precision of $0.9647$, recall of $0.9011$, and F1-score of
$0.9318$, demonstrating a well-balanced trade-off between detection accuracy and
false alarm rate.

\subsection{Score Distribution Analysis}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig_score_distribution.png}
\caption{Distribution of predicted probabilities for leak and normal classes.}
\label{fig:score_distribution}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:score_distribution} shows the distribution of predicted
probabilities for both classes. Leak samples tend to have higher predicted
scores, while normal samples are concentrated at lower values. The separation
between the two distributions indicates that the model effectively discriminates
between leak and non-leak conditions.

\subsection{Threshold Sensitivity Analysis}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig_threshold_sensitivity.png}
\caption{Performance metrics as a function of the decision threshold.}
\label{fig:threshold}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:threshold} illustrates how precision, recall, and F1-score vary
with the decision threshold. Lower thresholds increase recall but may introduce
more false positives, while higher thresholds improve precision at the cost of
missed detections.

The selected threshold $\tau = 0.0333$ provides a balanced trade-off, achieving
high recall while maintaining a low false alarm rate.

\subsection{Discussion}

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CNN-BiLSTM-Attention
architecture effectively captures both local and global temporal patterns in
multivariate sensor data.

Compared to earlier configurations, the final model significantly improves recall
while maintaining high precision and a low false alarm rate. The use of threshold
calibration plays a crucial role in achieving this balance, allowing the model to
adapt to different operational requirements.

Overall, the results confirm that the proposed approach is robust, reliable, and
well-suited for real-world leak detection scenarios where both detection accuracy
and operational efficiency are critical.
