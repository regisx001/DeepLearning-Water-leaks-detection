\section{Dataset and Exploratory Data Analysis}

\subsection{Dataset Description}

The dataset used in this study consists of multivariate time-series data
collected from an urban water distribution network over a monitoring period
of approximately three weeks (January 2024). The data includes measurements
from ten sensors capturing key hydraulic variables such as pressure and flow
rate, along with environmental variables including temperature.

After preprocessing and removal of anomalous burst events, the dataset contains
a total of 5{,}590 samples, among which 774 correspond to leak events,
representing approximately 13.8\% of the data. This class imbalance reflects
real-world conditions, where leak occurrences are relatively rare compared to
normal system operation.

Each observation is associated with a binary label indicating the presence or
absence of a leak. To enable supervised learning, the data is later transformed
into fixed-length sequences using a sliding-window approach.

\subsection{Distribution Analysis}

To better understand the characteristics of the data, we first analyze the
distribution of key variables for normal and leak conditions.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig1_pressure_distribution.png}
\caption{Pressure distribution under normal and leak conditions.}
\label{fig:pressure_distribution}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pressure_distribution}, pressure values during leak
events tend to be lower compared to normal operating conditions. This shift
reflects the physical behavior of the system, where leaks lead to pressure
drops in the network.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig2_flowrate_distribution.png}
\caption{Flow rate distribution under normal and leak conditions.}
\label{fig:flow_distribution}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:flow_distribution} shows that flow rate values are generally
higher and more dispersed during leak conditions. This behavior is consistent
with the expectation that leaks introduce additional flow demand, increasing
the variability of the signal.

\subsection{Temporal Behavior of Leak Events}

To analyze the temporal dynamics of the system, we examine sensor-level
time-series data with annotated leak events.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{images/fig3_timeseries_leak_sensor.png}
\caption{Time-series example from a sensor showing pressure and flow rate
variations with leak events highlighted.}
\label{fig:timeseries}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeseries} illustrates the temporal evolution of pressure and
flow rate for a representative sensor. Leak events are associated with noticeable
fluctuations, including pressure drops and irregular flow patterns. These
temporal dependencies highlight the need for sequence-based models capable of
capturing both short-term variations and long-term trends.

\subsection{Feature Correlation Analysis}

To assess relationships between variables, we compute the correlation matrix
of the main features.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig4_correlation_matrix.png}
\caption{Correlation matrix between features and leak status.}
\label{fig:correlation}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:correlation}, pressure exhibits a strong negative
correlation with leak status ($-0.606$), confirming its importance as a key
indicator of leaks. In contrast, flow rate shows a weaker positive correlation,
while temperature appears to have minimal direct influence on leak occurrence.

These observations motivate the use of feature engineering and deep learning
models to capture nonlinear interactions between variables.

\subsection{Discussion}

The exploratory analysis reveals several important characteristics of the dataset:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Leak events are associated with lower pressure and higher flow variability.
    \item Temporal patterns play a critical role in distinguishing leaks from normal operation.
    \item The dataset exhibits class imbalance, requiring appropriate evaluation metrics.
    \item Linear correlations alone are insufficient to fully characterize leak behavior.
\end{itemize}

These findings justify the use of a hybrid deep learning architecture capable of
modeling complex temporal dependencies and nonlinear feature interactions.
