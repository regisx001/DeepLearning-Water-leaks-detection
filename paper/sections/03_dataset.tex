\section{Dataset and Exploratory Data Analysis}

\subsection{Dataset Description}

The dataset used in this study consists of multivariate time-series data
collected from an urban water distribution network over a monitoring period
of approximately three weeks (January 2024). The data includes measurements
from ten sensors capturing key hydraulic variables such as pressure and flow
rate, along with environmental variables including temperature.

After preprocessing and removal of anomalous burst events, the dataset contains
a total of 5{,}590 samples, among which 774 correspond to leak events,
representing approximately 13.8\% of the data. This class imbalance reflects
real-world conditions, where leak occurrences are relatively rare compared to
normal system operation.

Each observation is associated with a binary label indicating the presence or
absence of a leak. To enable supervised learning, the data is subsequently
transformed into fixed-length sequences using a sliding-window approach.

\subsection{Distribution Analysis}

To better understand the characteristics of the data, we first analyze the
distribution of key variables under normal and leak conditions.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig1_pressure_distribution.png}
\caption{Pressure distribution under normal and leak conditions.}
\label{fig:pressure_distribution}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pressure_distribution}, pressure values during leak
events are systematically lower than those observed under normal operating
conditions. This shift reflects the underlying hydraulic behavior, where leaks
induce pressure drops within the network.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig2_flowrate_distribution.png}
\caption{Flow rate distribution under normal and leak conditions.}
\label{fig:flow_distribution}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:flow_distribution} shows that flow rate values exhibit higher
variability and a slight increase during leak conditions. This behavior is
consistent with the expected increase in water discharge associated with leaks,
leading to more dispersed flow patterns.

\subsection{Temporal Behavior of Leak Events}

To analyze temporal dynamics, we examine sensor-level time-series data with
annotated leak events.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{images/fig3_timeseries_leak_sensor.png}
\caption{Time-series example from a sensor showing pressure and flow rate
variations with leak events highlighted.}
\label{fig:timeseries}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:timeseries} illustrates the temporal evolution of pressure and
flow rate for a representative sensor. Leak events manifest as structured
temporal patterns, including sustained pressure drops and correlated flow
fluctuations. These observations highlight that leak detection cannot be treated
as a point-wise anomaly detection problem, but rather requires sequence-based
models capable of capturing temporal dependencies.

\subsection{Feature Correlation Analysis}

To quantify relationships between variables, we compute the correlation matrix
of the main features.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{images/fig4_correlation_matrix.png}
\caption{Correlation matrix between features and leak status.}
\label{fig:correlation}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:correlation}, pressure exhibits a strong negative
correlation with leak status ($-0.606$), confirming its role as a primary
indicator of leak events. Flow rate shows a weaker positive correlation,
while temperature has negligible correlation, indicating limited direct
influence on leak detection.

These results suggest that while individual features provide useful signals,
their relationships are inherently nonlinear and interdependent, motivating
the use of advanced learning models capable of capturing such interactions.

\subsection{Discussion}

The exploratory analysis reveals several key characteristics of the dataset:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Leak events are associated with lower pressure and increased flow variability.
    \item Temporal structure is essential for distinguishing leak patterns from normal behavior.
    \item The dataset exhibits significant class imbalance, requiring appropriate evaluation strategies.
    \item Linear relationships alone are insufficient to fully capture leak dynamics.
\end{itemize}

These findings justify the use of hybrid deep learning architectures and
sequence-based modeling approaches. In addition, they motivate the need for
robust evaluation and comparison with alternative models, as explored in the
subsequent sections.
